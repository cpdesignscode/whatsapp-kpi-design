01 — The Philosophy
What this dashboard is for
Every design decision flows from one sentence.
"Pulse is not a reporting tool — it's a signal. Every metric should reveal the one action a manager needs to take today, not confirm what they already suspected last week."
Design Principle · Christopher Praveen

Most analytics dashboards answer the wrong question. They answer "what happened?" when managers need to know "what do I do next?" Pulse is built around this distinction. Every layout choice, every metric placement, every alert threshold exists to move a manager from observation to action in as few seconds as possible.

The Brief
Design a WhatsApp Chat KPI dashboard for a SaaS platform built on the WhatsApp Business API. Primary users are customer support managers who need to monitor team performance, identify bottlenecks, and surface actionable insights from their WhatsApp conversation data.
What I Built
A 6-view interactive SaaS application covering Overview, Conversations, Agents, Topics, Reports, and Settings. All navigation is functional. Includes an AI Insight Pulse feature and a live natural-language query bar. Deployed as a public prototype.

Tools: Figma · HTML/CSS/JS · GitHub Pages

Core Design Tensions I Resolved

⚡
Data density vs. cognitive load
A KPI dashboard needs to show a lot. But showing everything equally means nothing stands out. I resolved this by establishing a strict 3-tier hierarchy: primary KPI cards (read in 2 seconds), secondary chart (read in 10 seconds), tertiary table (read on demand). Managers see what matters first; depth is always available but never imposed.
🎨
WhatsApp brand identity vs. professional SaaS aesthetic
WhatsApp's green carries instant brand recognition and psychological trust — the "green = good" metaphor is already wired into every user. But WhatsApp's consumer product is a messenger, not a business tool. I borrowed the dark palette and green accent, then stripped the messenger feel and rebuilt it as an enterprise dashboard. Familiar, but authoritative.
🤖
AI as noise vs. AI as signal
AI features in dashboards are often decorative — a "smart" label on a filter that doesn't change behaviour. I designed AI Insight Pulse to appear only on demand (no unsolicited AI content), to provide specific actionable language (not generic commentary), and to be dismissible in one click. The AI is opt-in, specific, and useful — or it stays invisible.

How it works
The ↗ insight button appears on every KPI card — hidden by default, surfaced on hover. When clicked, it expands an AI-generated 2-sentence analysis: first sentence gives context ("what this number means right now"), second gives an action ("what you should do about it"). The insight is specific, not generic — it references real data from the current session (which agent, which time window, which category).
The User Insight

Support managers in SMB companies check their dashboards during already-stressful moments — when something is going wrong. They don't have time to correlate a red metric with yesterday's data, identify the pattern, and reason to an action. They need the interpretation pre-done. AI Insight Pulse compresses a 5-minute analysis into a 10-second read.

Why This Product Specifically

WhatsApp Business operates in high-urgency, high-stakes contexts — customer complaints, time-sensitive orders, escalation chains. A 10-minute response delay can mean a lost sale or a lost customer. Unlike Zendesk or Intercom (which serve structured ticketing), WhatsApp is conversational and real-time. The feedback loop must be equally real-time. That's what AI Insight Pulse is designed for.

The AI Query Bar
✦ Ask Pulse — "Why is response time up?" · "Which agent needs help?" · "What's driving billing escalations?" ⌘K

A persistent natural-language query bar runs across every view. Managers can ask questions in plain English and receive instant, data-grounded answers — making the dashboard a two-way conversation, not a one-way report. The ⌘K shortcut makes it accessible from anywhere in the product without taking hands off the keyboard.

04 — Systems Thinking
Six views. One coherent product.
I designed the whole application, not just the hero screen. Every view is functional in the live prototype.
01
⬡
Overview
The command centre. Four primary KPIs, a 7-day volume chart with stacked bars for resolved/open/escalated, recent conversation table with live status, and a real-time alert strip. The screen a manager stares at during peak hours.
02
💬
Conversations
Full conversation log with filter pills by status (All / Open / Waiting / Resolved / Escalated). Each row shows contact, agent, topic, first response time, duration, and CSAT. Filterable by search. The operational view — what's happening right now.
03
👥
Agents
Per-agent performance cards with four metrics each: chats today, CSAT, avg response time, resolution rate. Online/offline status at a glance. Colour-coded warnings for underperforming agents. The team health view — who needs coaching vs. who deserves recognition.
04
◎
Topics
Category-level breakdown: Support, Sales, Billing, General — each with volume, response time, resolution rate, and CSAT. A bar segment shows relative volume at a glance. The strategic view — where to invest in templates, automation, or team training.
05
📋
Reports
Scheduled and manual report templates: Daily KPI Summary, Agent Performance, SLA Breach Log, CSAT Digest, Topic Trend Analysis. Each shows last-sent time, recipients, and a one-click export. The output view — what gets sent to stakeholders and executives.
06
⚙
Settings
Alert threshold configuration (response SLA, CSAT floor, escalation rate), integration management (WhatsApp Business API, Google Sheets, Slack, HubSpot), and team management (agents, roles, timezone, business hours). The foundation view — how the product is configured to serve a specific business.
How the Views Connect

Pulse is designed with a data flywheel: the Overview surfaces a problem → the Conversations view reveals who is affected → the Agents view shows whose queue is overloaded → Topics shows what category is driving it → Reports documents it for stakeholders → Settings lets you change the alert threshold so it surfaces earlier next time.

This isn't six separate screens. It's one continuous workflow for diagnosing, acting on, and preventing support team bottlenecks — built on WhatsApp Business data that most businesses are currently reading from a spreadsheet, if at all.

05 — What AI Can't Give You
Why a human designer made this better
AI tools can generate UI. They cannot make the decisions that make UI good.
1
Systems thinking, not screens
I designed the information architecture — what data lives in each view, how views connect, what a manager needs to accomplish in each one, and in what order. I designed state transitions: what happens when a filter is active, when an insight panel is open, when a row is filtered out. AI generates screenshots. It doesn't reason about workflows.
2
AI Insight Pulse is a product argument, not a feature
I identified that support managers use dashboards during high-stress, time-compressed moments, and that the missing layer is interpretation — not more data. I reasoned that WhatsApp's real-time conversational nature makes this more urgent than Zendesk or Intercom. Then I designed the feature to be opt-in, specific, and dismissible. That's product thinking. AI executes feature requests. It doesn't originate them.
3
Colour as a system, not a palette
I chose WhatsApp green (#25D366) as the positive delta colour because it exploits the existing mental model every user has from the WhatsApp messenger itself: green means delivered, green means good. The same green appears on resolved status chips, positive KPI deltas, and online agent indicators. Colour isn't decoration here — it's a communication system. That's a design decision, not a style choice.
4
A design philosophy you can hire
My principle — "Pulse is not a reporting tool — it's a signal" — is an intellectual position on what this product should be. It's specific enough that I can hold any design decision against it and know whether to keep it or cut it. Every layout choice in this document is defensible against that sentence. AI doesn't have positions. It has tendencies shaped by training data. I have a point of view.
